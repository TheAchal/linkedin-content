# Post for 2026-07-28

## Post text

Two Indian AI tutoring startups raised pre-seed money this week. YoLearn.ai closed $500K for a voice-first tutor in 22 languages. Lytmus AI raised 5 crore rupees to build AI mentors for NEET aspirants.

Good problem to chase. I built InfiNotes in the same space, past 10,000 users and profitable in five weeks on zero marketing, so I've lived what comes after the funding headline.

The model is never the hard part. Any team can wire an LLM to answer doubts. The hard part is week three, when the student has already gotten an answer and has to decide whether to open the app again without being told to.

An AI tutor is not a chatbot with a syllabus. It's a habit you earn daily, against a teenager's attention span and a hundred competing apps. Voice, more languages, a slick UI: none of that buys retention on their own. Only the feeling that the tool actually remembers where the student got stuck does.

I also shipped a NEET counselling product end to end, so I've watched this exact cohort closely. My advice to both founders: design for the second week before you design for the first.

What's your test for whether an AI product is actually sticky, or just well funded?
