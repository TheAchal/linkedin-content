# Post for 2026-07-29

## Post text

A Gujarat university fined two students 5,000 rupees each for hiding ChatGPT and Gemini inside smartwatches during an exam. Months later it caught six more the same way, doubled the fine and cancelled their papers. In between, it sent nearly 400 other students caught cheating to counselling instead of punishing them.

That is not a policy. That is an institution improvising, because the smartwatch was never the real problem.

I build AI study tools for a living, InfiNotes and an AI study companion I have not launched yet. The lesson I keep relearning: if a tool exists to hand a student the final answer, some will always smuggle it into the exam hall. Fining the smartwatch does not fix that.

The fix has to happen in the product, long before the exam. Build something that makes a student stronger at the subject, not just faster at the answer, and there is nothing left to sneak in. That is a harder product to build than a chatbot with a syllabus. It is also the only version that survives an exam hall.

Policy can only chase the symptom. The product decision closes the gap.

If you build AI study tools, where do you draw the line between helping a student learn and helping them finish?
